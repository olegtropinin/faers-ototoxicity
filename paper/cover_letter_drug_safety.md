Dear Editor,

I submit the original research article "Which drug–hearing-loss signals in FAERS survive adjustment
and independent replication? A disproportionality analysis of FAERS and Canada Vigilance" for
consideration in *Drug Safety*.

Several recent studies screened FAERS for drug-associated hearing loss with crude disproportionality
and reported many unlabelled signals. This study asks which of those signals survive. Using 19.9 million
deduplicated FAERS reports and 1.2 million Canada Vigilance reports, it evaluates crude and adjusted
detection against a label-derived reference set, replicates every signal in Canada Vigilance and over time,
and reviews the provenance of each robust unlabelled signal.

The main findings are methodological and, I believe, of direct interest to your readers:

- adjustment for indication, ototoxic co-medication and stimulated reporting changes about a fifth of the
  signal list but does not improve agreement with labels;
- replication in a second spontaneous-reporting database confirms most labelled associations but also
  "confirms" about one in five label-negative drugs, largely through solicited patient-support-programme
  reports, and co-medication bias replicates when crude estimates are compared;
- after adjusted replication and case-level review, only ten unlabelled signals remain, as hypotheses.

The analysis protocol was committed to a version-controlled repository before the first result was computed;
all deviations, including the post-hoc analyses, are dated and labelled. The study follows READUS-PV, and the
completed checklist is provided. Code, protocol and result tables are publicly available.
