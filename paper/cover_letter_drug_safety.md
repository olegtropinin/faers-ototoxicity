Dear Editor,

I submit the original research article "Adjustment and external replication of drug–hearing-loss signals:
a disproportionality analysis of FAERS and Canada Vigilance" for consideration in *Drug Safety*.

Several recent studies screened FAERS for drug-associated hearing loss with crude disproportionality and reported
many unlabelled signals. This study asks which of those signals survive. Using 19.9 million deduplicated FAERS reports
and 1.2 million Canada Vigilance reports, it compares crude and adjusted detection against a label-derived reference set,
replicates every signal in Canada Vigilance with a power-calibrated comparison, and reviews the report provenance of each
robust unlabelled signal, including an active-comparator check within the indication.

The main findings are methodological and, I believe, of direct interest to your readers:

- adjustment for indication, ototoxic co-medication and stimulated reporting changes the signal list but does not
  improve agreement with labels;
- labelled hearing-loss signals replicate in Canada Vigilance about as often as their FAERS strength predicts
  (observed/expected 1.02), whereas unlabelled signals replicate less than half as often (0.44; 0.26 when both databases
  are adjusted);
- solicited patient-support-programme reports, report clusters and shared co-medication produce spurious replications,
  so a second spontaneous-reporting database is informative only when both estimates are adjusted and these sources are
  handled explicitly;
- after these steps, seven of 22 robust unlabelled signals remain (two to nine under alternative post-hoc choices), as hypotheses.

The analysis protocol was committed to a version-controlled repository before the first result was computed; every later
change, including all post-hoc analyses, is dated and labelled. The study follows READUS-PV and the completed checklist is
provided (Supplementary Table S1). Code, protocol and result tables are publicly available.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. [Preprint: to be confirmed by the author.]
