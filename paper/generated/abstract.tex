\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Introduction:} Recent disproportionality analyses of the US Food and Drug Administration Adverse Event
Reporting System (FAERS) reported many drug--hearing-loss signals absent from labels. We assessed which survive adjustment for
hearing-specific biases and external replication.

\noindent\textbf{Methods:} Disproportionality analysis of 19.9 million deduplicated FAERS reports
(2004--2026) and 1.2 million Canada Vigilance reports, following a protocol committed before
analysis. For hearing loss and tinnitus, crude reporting odds ratios (signal: lower 95\% bound $>1$, $\geq$3 cases) were
compared with Mantel--Haenszel estimates stratified by age, sex and period after excluding ear-related indications, reports
naming established ototoxic drugs and lawyer reports. Methods were compared on a label-derived reference set (area under the
receiver operating characteristic curve, AUC) and by replication in Canada Vigilance, observed and power-calibrated; robust
unlabelled signals underwent case-by-case review.

\noindent\textbf{Results:} Adjustment changed the hearing-loss signal list (300 crude, 310
adjusted, 63 lost) but not agreement with labels (AUC 0.718 vs
0.723). Labelled signals replicated in Canada as often as predicted (observed/expected
1.02, 95\% CI 0.77--1.32);
unlabelled signals did not (0.44, 0.29--0.62;
post hoc, both databases adjusted: 0.26). Solicited patient-support-programme
reports, report clusters and shared co-medication produced spurious replications. Of 22 robust unlabelled signals (post
hoc), 15 were attributed to report clusters, stimulated reporting, indication or an established class; 7 remained,
led by pregabalin.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions:} Most unlabelled FAERS hearing-loss signals do not survive adjustment, adjusted replication and
provenance review. These findings are hypothesis-generating; cross-database replication needs adjusted estimates and explicit
handling of solicited reports.
\end{abstract}
